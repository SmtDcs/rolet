01 — the problem

Web3 games are slot machines with a wallet

  • Play-to-earn died because the fun was never real — the draw was the token, not the game
  • And when the draw is the draw — loot boxes, gacha, spin-to-win — skill has nowhere to sit
  • Players can feel the difference between a game that rewards them and a game that harvests them

“I wanted the opposite: a game where the money is real and the outcome is yours.”

02 — the game

Read the table, or pull the trigger

The chamber

  • 8 chambers — 5 live, 3 blank
  • 4 lives each; first to zero loses
  • 4 cards in hand, drawn from 12

The guarantee

  • Commit-reveal: your secret is hashed before the deal
  • Seed from a future Solana slot — nobody, including us, can grind it
  • The chambers are hidden, provably
03 — live

One prompt, then the whole match

  • Take a character — a Metaplex Core asset, free on devnet, ten matches of durability
  • Open a bench — the queue introduces two wallets
  • Approve once — a session key funds and registers itself; lobby, reveal, launch, every turn and the payout all sign with it
  • Play — pull the trigger, or play HawkEye and see the next chamber before you commit
  • Settle — 0.02 SOL from the program's treasury PDA to the winner

Live: web-production-40a6c.up.railway.app

05 — the economy

An economy that cannot inflate away

  • No token. Rewards are paid in SOL from a program-owned treasury PDA
  • A fixed reward — 0.02 SOL per match, the same whether the pot is rich or empty
  • Sinks are the game, not a decay curve: a character carries ten matches of durability and every bench spends one
  • The treasury pays out what players put in — nothing is minted to pay a winner

06 — what is real

What works, and what does not

Verified on devnet

  • A full match end to end: lobby, reveal, launch, turns on the rollup, settlement
  • 36 unit tests over the pure rules
  • One prompt per player per match, from a scripted two-wallet run

Not finished — and we say so first

  • The queue is a single in-memory process
  • claim_timeout cannot run while a match is delegated
  • Character ownership is self-attested at mint
  • Devnet only
07 — next

Where this goes

  • Next: mainnet, one queue store, a program-signed character mint, a timeout path that works on the rollup
  • Then: ranked seasons and a spectator view — the cards already make this a game people will watch
  • The bet: web2 proved a tight turn-based duel can hold millions of players. Nobody has moved that genre on-chain with the money handled honestly
